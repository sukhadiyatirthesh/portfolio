### Why is Google Shopping Feed Optimization Crucial for 2026 eCommerce?

People often ask me, "Tirthesh, why do you spend so much time on the product feed? Isn't bidding more important?" My answer is always the same: **Your product feed is the absolute foundation of your Google Shopping success.** Period. Without an optimized feed, even the most sophisticated bidding strategies will crumble. Google can't show your products effectively if it doesn't understand them. It’s that simple.

Think of it like this: your product feed is what tells Google exactly what you're selling, its price, availability, and a million other crucial details. This data then powers your Product Listing Ads (PLAs) across Search, Images, YouTube, Display, and even Gmail. A poor feed means poor visibility, irrelevant impressions, and ultimately, zero sales. In 2026, with competition fiercer than ever, your feed isn't just a requirement; it's your competitive edge.

#### The Anatomy of a Perfect Product Feed

A perfect product feed isn't just technically correct; it's strategically optimized for search queries and user intent. This means going beyond the basic requirements. We're talking about rich, descriptive, keyword-laden titles and descriptions that anticipate what your potential customers are typing into Google.

Here's the checklist I use for every client’s product feed:

*   **Unique ID:** Every product needs a unique `id`. Simple, but critical.
*   **Title:** This is arguably the *most important* attribute. It needs to be descriptive, include primary keywords, brand, key attributes (color, size, material), and any relevant model numbers. My format usually looks like: `Brand + Gender + Product Type + Key Feature + Color + Size/Variant`.
*   **Description:** Use longer-tail keywords here. Explain benefits, features, materials. Don't just copy-paste from your product page. Write for Google's algorithm *and* the user.
*   **Link:** Direct URL to the product page. Make sure it's correct and loads fast.
*   **Image Link:** High-quality, clear product images. Don't use watermarks. Test different angles. This influences CTR heavily.
*   **Price:** Accurate, up-to-date pricing. Mismatches lead to disapprovals.
*   **Availability:** `in stock`, `out of stock`, `preorder`. Keep it accurate to avoid frustrated customers.
*   **Brand:** Your brand name. Essential for brand-specific searches.
*   **GTIN/MPN/ISBN:** Global Trade Item Numbers (GTINs like UPCs, EANs, ISBNs) are vital for product matching and showing your ads alongside competitors. Google *loves* these.
*   **Google Product Category:** Categorize your products as specifically as possible using Google's taxonomy. This helps Google understand your product context.
*   **Product Type:** Your *internal* product categorization. This is incredibly powerful for segmenting campaigns. More on this later.
*   **Custom Labels (0-4):** Absolute game-changers. Use these for things like `profit_margin`, `seasonal_product`, `best_sellers`, `clearance`, `holiday_collection`, `new_arrival`. These unlock granular bidding.

#### Key Product Attributes You Can't Ignore

While all attributes are important, some have a disproportionate impact on **Google Ads Shopping campaigns** performance. Neglecting these is akin to setting money on fire.

1.  **Title:** As mentioned, this is your ad's headline. It dictates initial visibility.
    *   **Pro Tip:** Dynamically insert high-volume search terms. For example, if you sell "Running Shoes," and "men's black running shoes size 10" is a common search, ensure your titles reflect this specificity where possible.
    *   **Length:** Aim for 70-150 characters for maximum search visibility, but keep the most important info in the first 70 characters for mobile display.
2.  **Product Type:** Use your internal categorization to create specific ad groups and campaigns.
    *   Example: Instead of "Apparel," use "Apparel > Women's > Dresses > Evening Dresses." This lets you set distinct bids for different product categories.
3.  **Custom Labels:** This is where you get *really* smart with your optimization.
    *   Label 0: Profit Margin (`high_margin`, `medium_margin`, `low_margin`). Bid higher on high-margin products.
    *   Label 1: Performance (`best_seller`, `underperformer`, `new_product`). Push your best sellers, optimize underperformers.
    *   Label 2: Seasonality/Promotion (`summer_collection`, `diwali_sale`, `clearance`). Adjust bids based on demand.
    *   Label 3: Price Buckets (`under_₹1000`, `₹1000-₹5000`, `over_₹5000`). Different price points often mean different customer willingness to pay.
    *   Label 4: Brand Tier (`premium_brand`, `value_brand`).
    *   I've seen custom labels reduce CPA by **25%** just by focusing spend on high-margin products.

#### Leveraging Feed Rules for Dynamic Optimization

You don't need to manually edit your feed file every day. Google Merchant Center's Feed Rules are your best friend. They allow you to transform and optimize your product data *before* it gets used for your ads.

Here are common rules I implement for clients:

*   **Title Enrichment:** Append brand, color, size to existing titles. `[title] + " by " + [brand] + " - " + [color]`
*   **Product Type Mapping:** Map your internal categories to Google's product taxonomy if they don't match perfectly.
*   **Custom Label Creation:** Automatically assign custom labels based on product attributes like price, availability, or title keywords. For example, if a product description contains "leather," assign `custom_label_0` as "leather_goods."
*   **Attribute Exclusion:** Remove attributes that aren't necessary or might cause issues.
*   **Missing Attribute Defaults:** Set default values for `gender`, `age_group`, or `color` if they're often missing from your raw data.

> 💡 **PRO TIP:** Use `IF/THEN` statements in feed rules. For instance, `IF [price] > 5000 THEN set custom_label_0 to "high_value"`. This automates smart segmentation, saving you hours. Tools like Datafeedwatch or Channable can supercharge this, offering even more robust feed management capabilities.

### Mastering Google Shopping Bidding Strategies for Unmatched ROAS

Alright, your feed is immaculate. Now, how do you tell Google *how much* to pay for those precious clicks? This is where **bidding mastery** comes in. In 2026, Smart Bidding isn't just an option; it's often the most efficient way to achieve your ROAS goals, *provided* you feed it the right data and constraints.

#### Smart Bidding vs. Manual: When to Use What

This isn't an "either/or" debate anymore. It's about strategic application.

*   **Smart Bidding (Target ROAS, Maximize Conversion Value):**
    *   **When to Use:** For established campaigns with consistent conversion data (at least 15-20 conversions per month per campaign). Ideal for scaling and achieving specific ROAS targets. Google's AI can analyze millions of signals (device, location, time, audience, product attributes) in real-time, which no human can replicate.
    *   **Tirthesh's Take:** I rely heavily on tROAS. It's my go-to for most **Google Ads Shopping campaigns**. I start with an ambitious but realistic tROAS target, then slowly lower it if I need more volume, or raise it if I need better efficiency. Patience is key with tROAS; it needs a learning phase.
    *   **Prerequisite:** Accurate conversion tracking with conversion values is non-negotiable. If you don't have this, Smart Bidding won't work effectively.
*   **Manual CPC (with Enhanced CPC):**
    *   **When to Use:** For brand new campaigns with no conversion data, or for very niche products where conversion volume is extremely low. It gives you initial control. Enhanced CPC (eCPC) offers a hybrid approach, allowing Google to make slight bid adjustments to maximize conversions while you maintain manual control.
    *   **Tirthesh's Take:** I rarely use pure Manual CPC for Shopping. If I do, it's usually for a short test period (a few weeks) to gather initial data, then I switch to Smart Bidding as soon as I have enough conversions. eCPC is a decent stepping stone.

#### Crafting a Bid Strategy for Performance Max Integration

Performance Max (PMax) is Google's all-in-one campaign type, and it's heavily reliant on a strong product feed. While it automates much of the bidding, your underlying strategy *still matters*. PMax campaigns automatically pull in your product feed and use Smart Bidding.

*   **PMax and Shopping Overlap:** PMax will prioritize showing Shopping ads if it deems them most relevant. If you run both Standard Shopping and PMax, your PMax campaign can potentially cannibalize your Standard Shopping performance.
*   **The Strategy:** For most advertisers, moving a significant portion of their budget (or even all of it for certain products/goals) into PMax campaigns is the future. But you *must* provide it with excellent signals.
    *   **Audience Signals:** Don't skip these. Provide custom segments (based on your first-party data, competitor URLs, relevant keywords), remarketing lists, and customer match lists. This trains PMax on *who* to target. (Need a refresher on first-party data? Check out my guide: [Google Ads Audience Signals: Ultimate 2026 First-Party Data Guide](/blog/2026-10-01-google-ads-audience-signals-ultimate-2026-first-party-data-guide/))
    *   **Creative Assets:** Even for Shopping, PMax uses display and video assets. Provide high-quality images, videos, and headlines. This helps it expand reach beyond just product listings.
    *   **Conversion Value Rules:** These are crucial. If one conversion type (e.g., high-margin product sale) is more valuable than another (e.g., low-margin product sale), tell Google. This helps PMax optimize for what truly matters to your business's bottom line.

> ⚠️ **CRITICAL WARNING:** If you're running Standard Shopping and Performance Max side-by-side, be mindful of duplication. PMax aims to replace Standard Shopping for many use cases. Consider transitioning strategically, perhaps by moving only underperforming Standard Shopping campaigns to PMax first, or segmenting products.

#### Understanding Conversion Value Rules and Data Exclusions

These are advanced Smart Bidding tactics that can dramatically refine your ROAS.

*   **Conversion Value Rules:** Not all conversions are created equal. A customer buying a ₹10,000 product is more valuable than one buying a ₹500 product. With conversion value rules, you can dynamically adjust reported conversion values based on factors like:
    *   **Geographic location:** Customers from certain regions might be more valuable.
    *   **Device:** Mobile conversions might have a different effective value.
    *   **Audience:** Existing customers (from a remarketing list) might be assigned a higher value.
    *   **Product:** If specific product categories or custom labels (like `high_margin` products) are inherently more profitable, you can apply multipliers.
    *   **Example:** "If a conversion comes from `custom_label_0 = high_margin`, increase its reported value by 20%." This teaches Smart Bidding to bid more aggressively for those profitable sales.
*   **Data Exclusions:** Sometimes, your conversion data can be "noisy." This happens during promotional periods where conversion rates spike temporarily, or if there's a tracking error. Data exclusions tell Smart Bidding to ignore specific date ranges when learning, preventing it from making suboptimal future decisions based on unusual historical performance.
    *   **Example:** You ran a massive "Diwali Sale" with 50% off everything. Your conversion volume and ROAS likely skyrocketed for those few days. If you don't exclude this data, Smart Bidding might assume this level of performance is normal and bid too aggressively later, leading to poor ROAS. Exclude those dates from its learning.

### Advanced Google Shopping Campaign Structure: Beyond the Basics

Most people set up one Shopping campaign with all their products. That's a huge mistake. Granular campaign structure allows for granular control over bids, budgets, and negative keywords. This isn't just theory; it's how my clients achieve industry-leading ROAS.

#### Segmenting Products with Custom Labels for Granular Control

We talked about custom labels in the feed section. Now, let’s see how they translate into campaign structure.

*   **High-Profit/Low-Profit Segmentation:**
    *   Create a "High Margin Products" campaign. Target products with `custom_label_0 = high_margin`. Use an aggressive tROAS target.
    *   Create a "Low Margin Products" campaign. Target products with `custom_label_0 = low_margin`. Use a conservative tROAS target.
    *   **Result:** You're strategically maximizing profit, not just revenue. My campaigns often see a **20-30% improvement in overall account profitability** by segmenting like this.
*   **Best Sellers vs. New Arrivals vs. Clearance:**
    *   "Best Sellers" Campaign: Highest budget, aggressive tROAS.
    *   "New Arrivals" Campaign: Moderate budget, possibly Maximize Clicks or a slightly higher tROAS to gain initial visibility and data.
    *   "Clearance/Slow Movers" Campaign: Lower budget, specific tROAS, potentially lower bids to clear inventory.
*   **Product Type/Category Segmentation:**
    *   Beyond custom labels, use `product_type` to create separate campaigns for major product categories (e.g., "Electronics - Laptops," "Electronics - Smartphones"). This makes it easy to allocate budgets and set specific ROAS targets relevant to each category's profitability and market.

#### The Power of Query-Based Segmentation (N-GRAMs)

This is a more advanced tactic, but it's a goldmine for **Google Ads Shopping campaigns**. It involves using search query reports to identify how users are searching for your products and then structuring campaigns to control bids for those specific query types.

1.  **"Branded" Search Queries:** Users searching for your brand (e.g., "Tirthesh Brand Sneakers"). These are high-intent, high-conversion queries.
    *   **Strategy:** Create a dedicated "Branded Shopping Campaign." Bid very aggressively here (high tROAS, or even Max Conversions with a strong CPA goal). Use very broad negative keywords in this campaign to ensure *only* branded queries trigger it.
2.  **"Generic" Search Queries:** Users searching for product types without a specific brand (e.g., "men's running shoes"). These are still high-intent but broader.
    *   **Strategy:** Create a "Generic Shopping Campaign." Bid moderately. Add your brand name as a negative keyword here to push branded traffic to your dedicated branded campaign.
3.  **"Competitor" Search Queries:** Users searching for competitor brands (e.g., "Nike running shoes").
    *   **Strategy:** (Use with caution and legal awareness). Create a "Competitor Shopping Campaign." Bid conservatively, as ROAS might be lower, but it can be an acquisition channel. Add your brand and generic terms as negatives.
    *   **How it works:** In your generic and competitor campaigns, you'll negative out your branded terms. In your branded campaign, you'll negative out generic and competitor terms. This ensures query types flow to the campaign where you want to bid most aggressively or conservatively.

#### Strategic Use of Negative Keywords and Priority Settings

These two features are often overlooked, but they are absolutely essential for preventing wasted spend and guiding traffic efficiently.

*   **Negative Keywords:**
    *   **Campaign Level:** Apply broad negative keywords at the campaign level. For instance, in a "High-Margin" campaign, you might add terms like "cheap," "free," "review" (if reviews don't convert), or specific product types you *don't* want to show.
    *   **Ad Group Level:** For very specific control, add negatives to product group ad groups.
    *   **Example:** If you sell premium shoes, add "replica," "fake," "used." If you sell women's shoes, add "men's."
    *   **Ongoing Optimization:** Regularly check your Search Term Report (at least weekly!) to identify irrelevant queries and add them as negatives. This is how you continuously refine your campaigns. I often find new negatives that reduce irrelevant spend by **10-15%** monthly.
*   **Campaign Priority Settings (Low, Medium, High):**
    *   This is how you tell Google which campaign should "win" if multiple campaigns are eligible to show an ad for the same product.
    *   **Scenario:**
        *   **High Priority:** Your "Branded Shopping Campaign" (you want to always win for branded terms and bid aggressively).
        *   **Medium Priority:** Your "Generic Shopping Campaign" (for broader terms).
        *   **Low Priority:** Your "Clearance/Slow Movers Campaign" (you only want these to show if other campaigns aren't bidding, or for very niche terms).
    *   **Crucial Caveat:** Priority settings *only* matter if the products *overlap* across campaigns. If campaigns have completely distinct products, priority doesn't apply. Also, bid still plays a role. A low-priority campaign with a sky-high bid can still win over a high-priority campaign with a low bid. Priorities help *guide* Google's decision when bids are similar.

### Troubleshooting Common Google Shopping Campaign Issues & Boosting Performance

Even with the best setup, **Google Ads Shopping campaigns** can hit roadblocks. Knowing how to diagnose and fix these issues quickly is a hallmark of an elite marketer.

#### Diagnosing Feed Errors and Merchant Center Suspensions

Nothing kills a Shopping campaign faster than Merchant Center issues.

*   **Regular Checks:** Log into Google Merchant Center *daily* to check Diagnostics. Look for:
    *   **Product Disapprovals:** Fix `missing required attributes`, `invalid values`, `image issues`. These are often quick fixes via feed rules.
    *   **Account Warnings/Suspensions:** These are more serious. Often related to policy violations (misrepresentation, insecure checkout, misleading pricing). Address these immediately. Your whole account can be blocked.
*   **Price and Availability Mismatches:** This is a common one. Make sure your feed data perfectly matches your landing page. Implement automated updates (e.g., hourly pulls) from your eCommerce platform (Shopify, WooCommerce, Magento) to minimize these.
*   **Missing GTINs:** If you sell branded products and are missing GTINs, Google will throttle your impressions. Get them added. If you manufacture your own products and don't have GTINs, use your `brand` and `mpn` (Manufacturer Part Number).
*   **Website Crawl Errors:** Ensure your website is easily crawlable by Google. Use Google Search Console to check for errors. A non-crawlable site means Google can't verify product data, leading to disapprovals.

#### Combatting Ad Fatigue and Declining ROAS

Yes, even Shopping ads can experience fatigue, especially if your product set is small or your offers are stagnant.

*   **Creative Refresh (Images):** Test new product images. Different angles, lifestyle shots, or even videos can significantly boost CTR.
*   **Offer Optimization:** Are your prices competitive? Is your shipping free? Do you have compelling discounts? Your product feed is not just data; it's your *offer*.
*   **Search Term Refinement:** Continuously add negative keywords to filter out irrelevant searches. This ensures your ads are showing to the most qualified audience.
*   **Bid Adjustments:** If ROAS is declining, re-evaluate your bidding strategy. Is your tROAS target too ambitious? Or perhaps too conservative, causing you to lose valuable impressions?
*   **Audience Layering:** Add remarketing lists or customer match lists to your Shopping campaigns (even PMax). Bidding adjustments for warmer audiences can help boost ROAS.

#### The Impact of Seasonality and Promotional Events

Ignore seasonality at your peril. India has a unique eCommerce calendar, with festivals like Diwali, Holi, Eid, Christmas, and national holidays driving massive spikes in demand.

*   **Proactive Planning:** Months before a major event (e.g., Diwali), start preparing.
    *   **Feed Updates:** Ensure all promotional prices, new stock, and specific festival-themed product collections are updated in your feed and custom labels.
    *   **Budget Increases:** Allocate significant budget increases to relevant campaigns. I often increase budgets by **200-500%** during peak festival weeks.
    *   **Bid Adjustments:** For your "High Priority" and "Best Sellers" campaigns, increase your tROAS targets temporarily or use bid modifiers to ensure maximum visibility during peak hours/days.
    *   **Promotions:** Use Google Merchant Center Promotions to highlight discounts, free shipping, or special offers directly in your PLAs. This boosts CTR.
*   **Post-Event Cleanup:** After a promotion, revert budget and bid changes. Add negative keywords for expired offers ("Diwali Sale 2026"). This prevents wasted spend on old promotions.

> 💡 **PRO TIP:** Use `custom_label_2` for seasonal tags (e.g., `diwali_2026`, `christmas_sale`). Then, create specific campaigns or use bid modifiers based on these labels during promotional periods. This precision ensures you capitalize on seasonal demand effectively.

#### Key Metrics to Track Beyond ROAS

While ROAS (Return on Ad Spend) is king for **Google Ads Shopping campaigns**, it's not the *only* metric.

*   **Conversion Value / Cost:** This *is* ROAS, expressed differently. Focus on *net profit* if possible, not just revenue.
*   **Conversion Rate (CVR):** How many clicks turn into purchases? A low CVR might indicate an issue with your landing page, product offer, or targeting, even if your ROAS looks okay.
*   **Cost Per Acquisition (CPA):** How much does it cost to get one conversion? Crucial for understanding profitability, especially for high-volume, lower-ticket items.
*   **Click-Through Rate (CTR):** High CTR means your product titles and images are appealing. Low CTR? Time to optimize your feed's visual and textual elements.
*   **Average Order Value (AOV):** While not a direct campaign metric, it impacts your potential ROAS. Cross-reference AOV with campaign performance.
*   **Impression Share:** Are you missing out on potential customers? Low impression share means your bids or budget might be too low.
*   **Lost IS (Budget/Rank):** This tells you *why* you're losing impression share. Is it budget capping you? Or are your bids simply too low to compete?

#### Utilizing GA4 and Looker Studio for Deep Dives

Google Ads UI provides great tactical data, but for strategic insights and cross-channel analysis, you need GA4 and Looker Studio.

*   **Google Analytics 4 (GA4):**
    *   **User Behavior:** Track the entire user journey *after* the click. What pages do they visit? How long do they stay? Do they add to cart but not purchase? This helps identify funnel leaks.
    *   **Audience Insights:** GA4 gives you rich demographic and interest data about your Shopping campaign visitors. Use this to refine your audience signals in PMax or create custom segments.
    *   **Attribution:** Understand how Shopping campaigns contribute to conversions alongside other channels using GA4's data-driven attribution models.
*   **Looker Studio (formerly Google Data Studio):**
    *   **Custom Dashboards:** Build dashboards that combine Google Ads data with GA4, Google Merchant Center, and even your CRM/ERP data.
    *   **Trend Analysis:** Visualize performance trends over time, by product category, custom label, or geographic region. Identify patterns and anomalies quickly.
    *   **Reporting:** Create automated, digestible reports for clients or stakeholders, focusing on key KPIs and actionable insights.
    *   **Example:** I often create a Looker Studio dashboard that shows ROAS segmented by `custom_label_0` (profit margin) and `product_type`. This immediately highlights which product groups are driving profit and which need attention.

#### A/B Testing Your Shopping Campaigns

A/B testing isn't just for Search or Display. You can, and *should*, be testing in Shopping.

*   **Product Titles:** Create two versions of your feed with slightly different title structures for a subset of products. Run them in separate campaigns (or ad groups, using `item_id` exclusions) to see which drives higher CTR and ROAS.
*   **Images:** Test different primary images for your top-performing products.
*   **Bidding Strategies:** Test tROAS with a 300% target vs. Maximize Conversion Value with a target CPA in separate campaigns for similar product sets.
*   **Custom Labels:** Experiment with different ways of segmenting products using custom labels.
*   **Promotions:** A/B test different promotional messages.

> 💡 **PRO TIP:** When A/B testing, ensure you have enough data for statistical significance. Don't make decisions based on small sample sizes. Run tests for at least 2-4 weeks, or until you hit statistically relevant conversion numbers.

### The Future of Google Shopping Ads: What's Next in 2026?

The landscape of paid media is always shifting. For **Google Ads Shopping campaigns**, 2026 is all about automation, deeper AI integration, and leveraging *your* first-party data more effectively.

#### Performance Max: The Evolution of Shopping

PMax isn't going anywhere. If anything, it's becoming the default for comprehensive campaign management across Google's inventory.

*   **Strategic Adoption:** Don't fear PMax. Embrace it, but understand its mechanics. A strong product feed and rich audience signals are *essential* to guide PMax's AI effectively.
*   **Asset Groups:** Treat PMax asset groups like mini-campaigns. Optimize your creative assets within each group to ensure they're relevant to the products and audience signals you're feeding it.
*   **Focus on Inputs:** With PMax automating bids and placements, your job shifts to optimizing the *inputs*: the product feed, conversion tracking, conversion values, and audience signals. That's where you win.

#### The Role of AI and Automation

Google's AI is getting smarter, faster. It can analyze auction-time signals at a scale no human can.

*   **Beyond Bidding:** AI is influencing creative selection, audience targeting, and even which products are shown for specific queries.
*   **Data Quality is Key:** The mantra remains: "Garbage In, Garbage Out." If your product feed is messy, your conversion tracking is broken, or your conversion values are inaccurate, Google's AI will make suboptimal decisions, no matter how powerful it is. Your expertise shifts from manual adjustments to ensuring the AI has the best possible data to work with.

#### First-Party Data Integration

Privacy changes (like cookie deprecation) mean first-party data is becoming invaluable.

*   **Customer Match:** Upload your customer lists (emails, phone numbers) to Google Ads. Use these for remarketing, exclusions, and as audience signals for PMax. This is highly effective for driving repeat purchases and identifying valuable customer segments.
*   **Enhanced Conversions:** Implement Enhanced Conversions to improve the accuracy of your conversion tracking by sending hashed first-party data back to Google. This boosts Smart Bidding performance.
*   **CRM Integration:** Connect your CRM data to Google Ads via offline conversion tracking. This tells Google about the true long-term value of customers acquired through your campaigns, allowing Smart Bidding to optimize for deeper funnel metrics.

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1: How often should I update my Google Merchant Center product feed?**
A1: Your product feed should be updated as frequently as your product data changes, ideally at least once a day. For dynamic elements like price and availability, consider hourly updates or use Content API integrations for real-time synchronization to prevent disapprovals.

**Q2: Can I use custom labels to exclude certain products from my Google Shopping campaigns?**
A2: Yes, absolutely. You can assign a specific custom label (e.g., `custom_label_0` to `exclude`) to products you don't want to advertise, then create a product group in your Shopping campaign that specifically excludes items with that custom label.

**Q3: Is Performance Max replacing Standard Shopping campaigns entirely in 2026?**
A3: While Performance Max is Google's push towards consolidated campaign management, Standard Shopping campaigns still exist. However, PMax will prioritize eligible shopping inventory. Many advertisers are finding success by strategically transitioning, often using PMax for broad reach and Standard Shopping for highly granular control on specific product segments or query types where PMax lacks transparency.

**Q4: What's the best bidding strategy for a new Google Shopping campaign with no conversion data?**
A4: For new Google Shopping campaigns without conversion data, start with Maximize Clicks to gather initial traffic and data. Once you accrue at least 15-20 conversions within a campaign over a few weeks, transition to a Smart Bidding strategy like Maximize Conversion Value or Target ROAS, as they are far more efficient in the long run.

**Q5: How do I handle negative keywords for Google Shopping when I can't see specific search queries within Performance Max?**
A5: You can still add negative keywords at the account level, which will apply across all campaign types, including Performance Max. Additionally, keep refining your Standard Shopping campaigns' negative keyword lists, as those insights often apply broadly. Google also provides some search insights within PMax to guide overall targeting, though not at the granular query level.
